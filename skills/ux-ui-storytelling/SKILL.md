# UX/UI with storytelling

Great products tell one story, and every screen is a scene in it. Your job is
to design interfaces that work (UX), look intentional (UI), and keep the
narrative consistent from the first visit to the last confirmation.

## 1. Find the story before designing

Read the product's existing copy, landing page, README, `PRODUCT.md` or
`DESIGN.md`, and brand material. Write the story in five lines and keep it
visible while you work:

- **Hero:** the user, not the product. Who they are and their context.
- **Goal:** what they want to achieve, in their words.
- **Obstacle:** what makes it hard or scary today: money, time, trust,
  complexity.
- **Guide:** how the product helps, with its voice and personality.
- **Success:** what "done" feels like, and the proof they get.

If no story exists, propose one to the user before redesigning screens.

## 2. Map the journey as scenes

For the flow you are designing, list each step as a scene:

| Scene | User's question | What the screen must answer | Emotion to land |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | "Is this for me?" | Value in one sentence, for this hero | Recognition |
| Commitment | "Is it safe? How long?" | Trust signals, effort, what's needed | Confidence |
| Doing | "Am I doing it right?" | Progress, guidance, inline validation | Momentum |
| Waiting | "Is it working?" | Honest status, time estimate | Calm |
| Success | "Did it work? What now?" | Clear proof and the next step | Accomplishment |
| Failure | "What went wrong?" | Plain cause, the fix, nothing lost | Reassurance |

Every screen should answer its scene's question first and keep everything else
secondary.

## 3. Keep the narrative consistent

- **One voice:** the same tone, person ("tú" vs "usted"), and vocabulary
  everywhere. The same thing always has the same name, so build a small
  glossary and stick to it.
- **Continuity:** each screen picks up where the last one left off (titles
  echo the action that led there, as in "Pago enviado" after "Enviar pago").
  No dead ends, and every end opens the next chapter.
- **Visual narrative:** hierarchy, color, and motion support the plot. The
  primary action is the hero's next move. Use motion to show cause and
  effect, not decoration. Illustrations and imagery depict the hero.
- **Show progress:** steppers, checklists, and milestones make the journey
  visible and rewarding.
- **Moments that matter:** design the first run, the first success, empty
  states, and errors with extra care. They are where users decide to trust
  the product.
- **Honesty:** no dark patterns, fake urgency, or hidden costs. In finance and
  security flows, clarity beats cleverness.

## 4. Craft (UX + UI)

Apply the ux-design skill for flows, states, forms, and accessibility, and the
ui-design skill for layout, typography, color, tokens, and responsive
behavior. Use impeccable for deep critique and polish. Accessibility is part
of the story too: everyone must be able to finish it.

## 5. Deliverables

- The five-line story and the scene map for the flow.
- The screens or code changes, with copy written for the story. Microcopy is
  part of the design, not a placeholder.
- A short critique of the current state: where the story breaks (inconsistent
  names, abrupt endings, missing feedback, off-voice copy), ranked by impact,
  each with a concrete fix.
- When you change UI, verify it visually (screenshot at mobile and desktop
  widths, light and dark) and check it against the story before saying done.
